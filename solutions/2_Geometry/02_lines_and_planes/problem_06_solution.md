
# Exercise 6 – Angle Between Planes

## Question

Given:

Π₁: x + y + z = 1

Π₂: x - y = 2

First use the normal vectors to decide whether the planes are parallel.

If not, compute the angle between them and explain why the normal vectors alone are enough for this calculation.

## Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Normal Vectors

The normal vector of a plane is obtained from the coefficients of x, y, and z.

For Π₁:

n₁ = (1, 1, 1)

For Π₂:

n₂ = (1, -1, 0)

### Step 2: Check Whether the Planes Are Parallel

Two planes are parallel if their normal vectors are scalar multiples of each other.

Here:

n₁ = (1, 1, 1)

n₂ = (1, -1, 0)

These vectors are not scalar multiples.

Therefore, the planes are not parallel.

### Step 3: Calculate the Dot Product

The angle between the normal vectors is calculated using:

cos(θ) = |n₁ · n₂| / (||n₁|| ||n₂||)

First, calculate the dot product:

n₁ · n₂ = (1)(1) + (1)(-1) + (1)(0)

n₁ · n₂ = 1 - 1 + 0

n₁ · n₂ = 0

### Step 4: Calculate the Magnitudes

||n₁|| = √(1² + 1² + 1²)

||n₁|| = √3

||n₂|| = √(1² + (-1)² + 0²)

||n₂|| = √2

### Step 5: Calculate the Angle

cos(θ) = |0| / (√3 × √2)

cos(θ) = 0

θ = arccos(0)

θ = 90°

Therefore, the planes are perpendicular.

### Step 6: Explain Why Normal Vectors Are Enough

A normal vector determines the orientation of a plane.

The angle between two planes is equal to the acute angle between their normal vectors.

Therefore, we only need the normal vectors to calculate the angle.

## Final Answer

Normal vectors:

n₁ = (1, 1, 1)

n₂ = (1, -1, 0)

The planes are not parallel.

The angle between the planes is:

θ = 90°

Therefore, the two planes are perpendicular.
