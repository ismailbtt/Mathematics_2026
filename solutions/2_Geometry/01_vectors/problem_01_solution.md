
# Exercise 1 – Vector Operations and Their Meaning

Given:

u = (2, -1, 3)

v = (-1, 4, 2)

## 1. Calculate u + v

We add the corresponding components:

u + v = (2 + (-1), -1 + 4, 3 + 2)

**Result:**

u + v = (1, 3, 5)

## 2. Calculate u - v

We subtract the corresponding components:

u - v = (2 - (-1), -1 - 4, 3 - 2)

**Result:**

u - v = (3, -5, 1)

## 3. Calculate 3u - 2v

First, multiply the vectors by their scalars:

3u = 3(2, -1, 3) = (6, -3, 9)

2v = 2(-1, 4, 2) = (-2, 8, 4)

Now subtract:

3u - 2v = (6, -3, 9) - (-2, 8, 4)

3u - 2v = (8, -11, 5)

**Result:**

3u - 2v = (8, -11, 5)

## 4. Verify that (u + v) - v = u

We know that:

u + v = (1, 3, 5)

Now subtract v:

(u + v) - v = (1, 3, 5) - (-1, 4, 2)

= (2, -1, 3)

Since u = (2, -1, 3), the identity is verified.

**Result:**

(u + v) - v = u

## 5. Geometric Interpretation

Adding two vectors means placing the starting point of the second vector at the endpoint of the first vector.

The resulting vector connects the starting point of the first vector to the endpoint of the second vector.

This is called the Triangle Rule of Vector Addition.

Vector addition can also be represented using the Parallelogram Rule.

- u - v = (3, -5, 1)
- 3u - 2v = (8, -11, 5)
